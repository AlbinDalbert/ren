Most applications behave as if they are the permanent centre of your work. Your writing, links, and careful structure become implementation details inside a database you rarely see.

I want to begin somewhere else: **the artifact is the important part**. The application is only one instrument for working with it.

## What durability means

Durable does not have to mean primitive. Plain files can still participate in rich interfaces, graphs, search indexes, validation, and careful editing environments. The distinction is that those capabilities are built *around* the material rather than used to hold it hostage.

A durable artifact should remain:

- inspectable without the original application;
- movable with ordinary tools;
- understandable enough to repair;
- useful in a smaller, simpler context.

This is one reason I keep returning to HTML. It is imperfect, wonderfully ordinary, and capable of carrying both a document and the links that locate it in a larger body of work.

## The interface still matters

None of this is an argument against good interfaces. In fact, respecting the artifact makes interface work more interesting. The editor can become calmer and more opinionated because it does not also need to pretend it is the only doorway into your own material.

The tool can help, then get out of the way. The work remains.
