“Calm” is often translated into pale colours, generous radii, and very little information. That can be pleasant, but it is not quite what I mean by quiet software.

A quiet tool may be dense. It may show paths, status, timestamps, and structure. What makes it quiet is that this information has a hierarchy and a reason to be present.

## Attention is part of the design

Every interface makes a claim on attention. Animation, colour, alerts, labels, and empty space all tell us where to look and how urgently to look there.

The goal is not to remove character. It is to spend character deliberately:

- one signal colour instead of five competing accents;
- motion that explains a transition rather than decorating it;
- metadata where it builds trust;
- typography that gives the work a voice without speaking over it.

Quiet software is allowed to be distinctive. It simply knows that the person came to do something other than admire the interface.
